5 Tennis Practice Habits That Actually Make You Better

A lot of practice time gets wasted because players hit a lot of balls without solving a real problem. Better tennis practice usually comes from better structure. The goal is not to look busy. The goal is to leave the court with one or two improvements that are more likely to hold up next time.

Shot AIReview the patterns behind your best and worst practice reps.
  1. Start each session with one purpose. Choose a single focus like spacing, serve rhythm, or rally depth.
  2. Track something concrete. Count rally length, first-serve percentage, or how many balls land past the service line.
  3. Alternate drills and live points. The skill should survive outside the drill if it is going to matter.
  4. Film short samples of your real strokes. Practice feels can be misleading without video.
  5. End with a clear review. Decide what improved, what stayed shaky, and what the next session should target.

Why these habits matter

They all reduce randomness. Instead of judging the day by mood, you start judging it by evidence. That makes improvement less emotional and more repeatable, especially for recreational players who do not get coached constantly.
