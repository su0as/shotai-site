Shot AI vs SwingVision: What's Actually Different?

Both Shot AI and SwingVision are legitimate tools for tennis and pickleball players who want more from their practice sessions. But they are built for fundamentally different purposes, and choosing the wrong one means paying for features you will not use. Here is an honest breakdown of what each does well, what each does not, and who should use which.

What SwingVision is built for

SwingVision is primarily a match analysis and stats platform. Its core features are automated score tracking, line calling, shot detection, and match video with auto-generated highlights. It is designed to answer questions like: how many winners did I hit, what was my first serve percentage, where are my groundstrokes landing on the court?

It requires a fixed camera position — typically mounted behind the court or on a tripod at the back fence — to do its automatic shot detection and line calling. The technology is impressive and the stats it produces are genuinely useful for players who want match-level data. SwingVision is strongest when you are playing full matches and want a comprehensive record of what happened.

What Shot AI is built for

Shot AI is a swing mechanics coaching tool. Its core feature is 17-point body tracking that analyzes your joint positions, paddle or racket path, contact point, and follow-through frame by frame. It answers different questions: is my backswing too long, where am I making contact relative to my hip, is my follow-through going where I intend?

Shot AI also covers golf — something SwingVision does not do at all. For multi-sport athletes who play two or three of these sports, that matters. It also works without any fixed equipment: point your iPhone at a wall, a court, or a practice area and film yourself. No court mounting, no setup beyond a tripod.

The real difference: practice vs match analysis

This is the clearest way to think about it. SwingVision is a match analysis tool that you use after games to understand what happened statistically. Shot AI is a practice coaching tool that you use during drills to understand what your body is doing mechanically.

If you already know what your errors are but want data on how often they happen in matches, SwingVision is the right fit. If you know you have errors but are not sure exactly what is causing them — and want help fixing the movement pattern — Shot AI is the right fit.

Some players use both: Shot AI during practice sessions to fix mechanics, SwingVision during matches to track whether the improvements are showing up in their stats. They answer different questions and they do not overlap much.

Equipment and setup requirements

SwingVision works best with a fixed camera position — a tripod at the back fence or a court-mounted setup. This is not complex, but it does mean you need to plan ahead and set up before play. Line calling and shot detection depend on a consistent camera position relative to the court.

Shot AI works from any angle with any iPhone. You can set it up in 30 seconds for a practice drill, move it to a different angle for a different shot, and use it at a wall, a range, or a court without any pre-configured setup. There is no calibration step.

Who should use Shot AI

  • Players who want to fix specific technique problems (backswing, contact point, follow-through)
  • Golfers who also play tennis or pickleball and want one app for all three sports
  • Solo practitioners who drill without a partner and want coaching feedback
  • Players who do not have access to a fixed court or who practice in different locations
  • Players earlier in their development who need mechanics help more than match stats

Who should use SwingVision

  • Competitive players who play regular matches and want detailed stats on serve, rally, and shot placement
  • Players who want automatic line calling and video highlights from matches
  • Tennis and pickleball players who already have solid mechanics and want to optimize strategy and patterns
  • Players who play in a fixed venue where a mounted camera setup makes sense

Bottom line

SwingVision and Shot AI are not really competing for the same use case. They are different tools for different stages and types of practice. If you are primarily working on technique and want AI coaching feedback on your mechanics, Shot AI is the right choice. If you are primarily playing matches and want stats, highlights, and line calling, SwingVision is the right choice.
